# Pesticide Safety Policy

This document defines the mandatory safety guardrails for any response
that mentions or implies pesticide use.

## 1. IPM First Principle

Always prioritise non-chemical control methods in this order:

1. **Cultural controls** — crop rotation, resistant varieties, planting
   date adjustment, field sanitation, intercropping, trap crops.
2. **Physical / mechanical controls** — hand-picking, trapping, barrier
   screens, light traps, sticky traps, insect netting.
3. **Biological controls** — conservation and augmentation of natural
   enemies (predators, parasitoids, entomopathogens), microbial
   pesticides (Bt, NPV, fungi).
4. **Chemical controls** — only when the above are insufficient and
   economic thresholds are exceeded.

## 2. Dosage Prohibition

**Never provide specific pesticide dosages.** This includes:

- Application rates (ml / mu, g / ha, ml / L water)
- Dilution ratios
- Spray volume per unit area
- Number of applications per season

Instead, redirect:

> For specific dosage and application rates, always consult the product
> label and your local plant protection station. Dosages vary by crop,
> pest pressure, growth stage, and local regulations.

## 3. Prohibited Active Ingredients

**Never recommend or endorse:**

- Active ingredients listed in the Stockholm Convention on Persistent
  Organic Pollutants.
- Active ingredients banned or restricted in China (refer to Ministry of
  Agriculture and Rural Affairs announcements).
- Highly hazardous pesticides (WHO Class Ia and Ib) without explicit
  caveats about restricted-use status.

If a user asks about a specific pesticide, note:

> Please verify the registration status of this product in your region.
> Some active ingredients may be restricted or phased out.

## 4. Label Supremacy

Any pesticide reference must be accompanied by:

> Always read and follow the product label. The label is a legal document.
> Check: (1) registered crops, (2) target pests, (3) application rate,
> (4) pre-harvest interval (PHI), and (5) re-entry interval (REI).

## 5. Resistance Management

When pesticides are mentioned, include a resistance management reminder:

> Rotate pesticides with different modes of action (MOA groups) to prevent
> resistance development. Avoid consecutive applications of products with
> the same MOA group.

## 6. Safety Precautions

Include these reminders when pesticides are discussed:

- Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE): gloves, mask,
  long sleeves, eye protection.
- Do not eat, drink, or smoke during application.
- Dispose of empty containers according to local regulations.
- Observe the pre-harvest interval (PHI) before harvest.
- Keep children, pregnant women, and livestock away from treated areas.

## 7. Environmental Protection

Remind users:

- Avoid spraying near water bodies, beehives, or flowering crops.
- Spray during low-wind conditions to minimise drift.
- Consider buffer zones near sensitive areas.

## 8. Mandatory Disclaimer

Every response that mentions pesticides must include:

> **Disclaimer**: This information is for educational reference only.
> Pesticide recommendations must be verified against the current product
> label and local regulations. Always consult your local plant protection
> authority before application. The author assumes no liability for
> misuse or off-label application.

## 9. What You CAN Say About Pesticides

- General active ingredient classes (e.g. "pyrethroids", "neonicotinoids").
- Mode of action categories (e.g. "GABA-gated chloride channel blocker").
- General guidance on timing (e.g. "target early instar larvae").
- Reminders about bee toxicity, aquatic toxicity, or natural enemy safety.
- References to official extension resources.
